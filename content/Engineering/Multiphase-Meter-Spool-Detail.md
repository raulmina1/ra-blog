# Red Eye Metering — where the design decides whether the measurement tells the truth

> A multiphase meter reads oil, water and gas in a single pass — no separator required. But it only reads honestly if the spool that hosts it is designed right. That design detail is exactly the service RA provides.

## What Is a Multiphase Meter?

A **multiphase meter** measures the fractions of oil, water and gas flowing through a wellhead or pipeline **at the same time, without separating them first**. This is a huge advantage over traditional three-phase separators: no bulky vessel, no long residence time, no sampling lag. The operator gets a **continuous, real-time read** of what a well is actually producing.

One of the most proven technologies for this is the **Red Eye** family of instruments — **water-cut meters** that determine the amount of water in the crude stream **using near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy**. The meter shines light through the flowing fluid and reads how much is absorbed at specific wavelengths; because water, oil and gas absorb differently, the instrument computes the water cut on the fly.

## What Makes the Measurement Honest

The optics are only as good as the conditions around them. A Red Eye meter will give accurate readings **only if**:

- **the flow is stable and well developed** when it reaches the sensing window — turbulence, swirling or separation upstream corrupts the reading;
- a **straight, straight-run section** of pipe lets the stream even out before the sensor;
- the **meter is mounted at the right height and orientation** for the fluid to pass cleanly;
- **no stray vibration or thermal stress** distorts the spool and the alignment of the optics.

A bad design can quietly turn an expensive, accurate instrument into one that reports attractive numbers — for weeks — before anyone notices the error. That is why the **spool that hosts the meter is engineering, not plumbing**.

## The Spool: The Meter's Home

The drawing on this page is a **spool designed to mount a multiphase water-cut meter**. It is a short, pre-fabricated section of pipe with **flanges on both ends**, built in the shop and bolted into the line in the field:

- **VISTA FRONTAL — UBICACIÓN DE CONEXIONES** (front view): where every instrument and process connection sits, so the shop builds it exactly once.
- **WELDOLET DETALLE A**: the welded branch connection for the meter's tie-in — a weldolet chosen over a plain cut-and-weld tee because it **distributes stress better under internal pressure**.
- **Dual-unit dimensions** in **inches and millimetres** (total height 7.359 in / 186.944 mm; width 22.25 in / 565.15 mm) — the standard for international fabrication and QA.

The weld geometry, the branch reinforcement, the flange classes, the connection callouts: **every one of these decisions is what a fabrication detail drawing protects.** Get the detail right and the instrument sees clean, stable flow and reads true. Get it wrong and the point of failure — and the point of error — is hidden for as long as the data keeps flowing.

## Why It Matters Commercially

- **Fiscal and operational metering** runs on this measurement. Operators bill, allocate and decide on it.
- A **few percent of water-cut error** on a high-rate well can mean the difference between "profitable" and "not" — or between two partners reporting differently on the same volume.
- **Commissioning and QA** depend on a spool being dimensionally true to spec, because the meter is only as repeatable as the geometry that holds it.
