## ⚡ Brent slips to \$108.82 on Russian supply relief after closing Friday at \$109.86, its highest since 19 May

Brent settled \$3.94, or 3.7 %, higher at \$109.86 on Friday — its highest close since 19 May and +4.9 % on the week — and WTI settled \$101.41 (+3.5 %, +10.4 % on the week); in Sunday electronic trade Brent was \$108.82 and WTI \$101.15, and early Monday in Asia Brent fell a further \$1.06 to \$108.80 after Trump's sanctions waiver.

**Why it matters.** The market is now trading two offsetting risks with no buffer behind either: ~509m barrels of inventories below pre-war levels and record diesel prices against a waiver that adds hours, not weeks, of supply. The number to watch is not Brent's close but the diesel crack — that is what feeds European gas, electricity and headline inflation.

### 🤖 AI & Models

**The capex suppliers are printing records while the AI equity trade reprices: ASML €11.7bn quarter, TSMC +50.9 %**

ASML reported Q3 net sales of €11.7bn against €9.3bn in Q2 with the gross margin up to 57.5 % from 54.0 %, and TSMC revenue rose 50.9 % year on year — both record quarters — while consensus now implies Q3-2026 operating EPS growth of 24.0 % for the S&P 500.

*Why it matters:* This is the IMF's circularity warning arriving as earnings: the physical build-out has never been stronger, the financing of it has never been more expensive.

### ⚡ Energy & Markets

**German gas: 60.5 % in storage, TTF above €85, new-customer tariffs up from 8 to 12.4 ct/kWh — and analysts split between €50 and €130 for 2027**

German storage stood at 60.5 % on 16 October — 15 points below last year and far from the 80 % target — while TTF traded above €85/MWh in mid-October against ~€32 before the war.

*Why it matters:* The distribution of outcomes is the message: a €50–130 range for next winter is not a forecast, it is a statement that European gas has become a binary bet on Hormuz and Qatar.

### 📊 Money & Policy

**Gross debt reaches COP 1,068 trillion as the 2027 budget deadline hits Tuesday midnight**

Colombia's gross national debt reached COP 1,068 trillion by August 2026, of which COP 818.81 trillion (76.6 %) is domestic and COP 249.52 trillion (23.4 %) external, per Contraloría data reported by El Tiempo.

*Why it matters:* The debt number is what turns "unsustainable" from an IMF opinion into arithmetic: a quarter of the budget goes to debt service, and the budget must be passed this week.

<sub>El Tiempo (via El País) / Infobae (CARF) / El Heraldo / Pluralidad Z (DANE) · 18.10.2026 — [read the source](https://www.elpais.com.co/economia/las-finanzas-en-el-gobierno-petro-generaron-que-la-deuda-bruta-superara-el-billon-de-pesos-hasta-agosto-de-2026-1823.html)</sub>

**The peso ends the week at COP 3,174.67 and is up more than 22 % this year — and the coffee belt is paying for it**

The TRM in force on 19 October is COP 3,174.67, after the spot closed Friday at COP 3,174.72; the peso is down COP 138 in October (−4.17 % from 3,312.84 on 1 October), −15.6 % for the year and more than 22 % stronger against the dollar in 2026.

*Why it matters:* A 22 % appreciation with a 6 % real rate is a carry trade that punishes exactly the sector the government needs — exporters — while flattering the inflation headline.

**A Fed blackout, a hot 3.7 % CPI and Tesla plus Meta on the same afternoon: the week that decides the October meeting**

The Fed entered its pre-meeting blackout at midnight on Saturday 17.10. and stays silent until 29.10., ahead of the 27–28 October FOMC; the last word was Chair Kevin Warsh on Friday insisting "I don't prejudge meetings".

*Why it matters:* Two conflicting channels meet this week: oil above \$109 pushing inflation up, and AI-credit stress pulling valuations down, with no Fed voice to arbitrate.
