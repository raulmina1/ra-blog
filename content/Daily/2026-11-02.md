## ⚡ DIW cuts Germany to 1.0 % — and its oil assumption is already 37 dollars below spot

DIW expects German growth of 1.0 % in 2026 and 1.4 % in 2027 (−0.3 and −0.2 pp versus its winter forecast), inflation 2.4 % / 2.3 %, and no further ECB hikes. The forecast assumes oil at about \$75/bbl in 2026 and \$68 in 2027 and gas at €46 then €32/MWh — both derived from futures.

**Why it matters.** Read the assumptions against the tape: Brent is at \$112 and TTF at €102, so the forecast is a bet that the second quarter of 2027 brings the de-escalation. If Hormuz stays shut past the capacity review on 29 November, the growth number is optimistic rather than central — and the ECB's December meeting becomes a hike, not a hold.

## In brief

### 🤖 AI & Models

**The AI-capex story gets its first positive cash-flow counterexample — Microsoft**

Microsoft's September quarter: revenue \$92.3bn (+19 %), Microsoft Cloud \$63.9bn (+30 %), Azure +48 %, operating income \$45.9bn (+21 %), free cash flow +\$18.6bn with capex cash paid of \$39.4bn.

*Why it matters:* Alphabet (−\$9.07bn FCF) and Amazon (−\$42.4bn) both went cash-negative this quarter; Microsoft did not, because it bills seats and credits while spreading leases.



### ⚡ Energy & Markets

**Brent slides 1.3 % on Doha, not on barrels**

Brent fell \$1.51 (−1.33 %) to \$111.92 and WTI \$1.78 (−1.71 %) to \$102.34 by 23:03 GMT on Monday, with Iranian and Qatari officials in a third straight day of talks. Reuters' move implies a Friday close near \$113.4 for the front month; some Friday reports quoted \$117.64 for a later-dated contract.

*Why it matters:* Physical supply has not changed: Hormuz is at 2 transits a day and inventories are depleted.



**Germany enters winter with 61 % in storage — and a regulator arguing that the target no longer matters**

At the 1 November statutory date German storage stood at about 61.2 % against an effective requirement of 70 % (80 % for most caverns, 45 % for six).

*Why it matters:* "No supply crisis, a price crisis" (Hendrik Diers, Cologne) is the right frame: the binding constraint is not molecules but the cost of topping up from a spot market squeezed by Hormuz.



**TTF crosses €100 — the gas market is repricing faster than oil**

Dutch TTF traded at €102/MWh (−1.8 %) on Monday morning after touching a 12-month high of €105 on 30 October; that is +36.6 % in a month and +82.6 % in three. The move came in the same week storage missed its target and Qatar's LNG carrier was attacked in the Gulf.

*Why it matters:* Gas is now the faster-moving channel of the energy shock: oil carries a war premium that diplomacy can shave, gas carries a winter-risk premium that only weather can remove.



### 📊 Money & Policy

**A holiday Monday, a peso at 3,182.99 and a central bank that will not move first**

TRM \$3,182.99, unchanged into a week with only four trading days (Monday 2 November is a public holiday); analysts see \$3,160–3,250 with an opening bias toward the top of the band.

*Why it matters:* The peso's October strength came from the rate differential, and that support erodes precisely now: BanRep is on hold while the Fed is priced to hike in December.



### 🌍 Geopolitics & Conflict

**The IRGC says two tankers hit mines in Hormuz — the claim is still uncorroborated, and traffic is at 2 transits a day**

On Sunday the IRGC navy said two "violating" tankers, one a supertanker, exploded after striking mines on an unauthorised route; one was burning, both had AIS off, no names and no casualty figures were given, and no maritime authority has confirmed it.

*Why it matters:* Mines are a slow-acting insurance event, not a spot-price event: they raise war-risk premia and force night-running, while Gulf producers keep lifting cargoes near pre-war levels on quiet routes.
