## ⚡ Brent falls 7.6 % in a day — the biggest drop in over three months, on words not barrels

Monday's settlement: Brent \$104.77, −\$8.66 (−7.63 %), and WTI \$96.46, −\$7.66 (−7.36 %) — the steepest single-day fall since the escalation began. Intraday lows were \$103.28 and \$95.15.

**Why it matters.** This is the mirror image of the 31 October crash: a premium being removed by diplomacy while physical supply is unchanged — Hormuz traffic is still a fraction of the pre-war 85 transits a day.

### 🤖 AI & Models

**Anthropic stops agents from re-sending the whole conversation — 35 % faster first token, same bill**

Message threads is in public beta in the Messages API: the first request sends the full conversation plus system prompt and tools, every later turn sends only the new messages and the previous response id, and Anthropic rebuilds the context server-side.

### ⚡ Energy & Markets

**Goldman raises its Brent forecast to \$105 — and models \$140 if the Gulf stays impaired**

Goldman lifted its December Brent forecast to \$105 from \$90 and WTI to \$94 from \$81.

*Why it matters:* The forecast and the tape disagree by almost nothing today (\$105 vs \$104.77), which is unusual and tells you the desks now treat the ceasefire as the tail risk and the status quo as the base case.



**Reiche's fuel-price cap would be triggered by neighbours, not by an absolute price**

The first draft from Economy Minister Katherina Reiche's ministry would create the legal basis for a fuel price cap without introducing one: the cap activates only if German petrol and diesel exceed the average price increases of the other EU states by more than 5 percentage points in three.

*Why it matters:* This is a relative cap, which means it cannot lower prices if the whole continent is expensive — precisely the current case.



**European gas gives back 9 % — but the winter curve still prices above €100**

Front-month TTF fell 5.1 % to €98.26/MWh on Monday (earlier −3 % at €101, intraday reports of almost −9 % in futures) after peaking above €104 at the end of October; NBP −3.2 %. The rally had been the strongest monthly gain at continental hubs since 2022 (+34–36 % in October).

*Why it matters:* The prompt contract is now a diplomacy derivative, while the winter curve is a storage derivative — and they disagree.



### 📊 Money & Policy

**The rescue bill reaches the floor — and the government starts using the word "default"**

Debate on the Ley de Rescate Económico (Bill 477, 105 articles) reached the Cámara plenary on Monday.

*Why it matters:* The bill's political problem is arithmetic, not ideology: it must raise at least COP 44 trillion while its own modelling admits a 0.6–0.8 pp hit to the cost of living, and it lands on the deciles least able to absorb it.



### 🌍 Geopolitics & Conflict

**The US struck Qeshm — defensive, undisclosed, and hours before the vote**

US officials confirmed to the WSJ, and then to Reuters and AP, that the US attacked Qeshm Island on Monday — the largest island in the Strait of Hormuz, home to an underground missile facility, a naval base and a major desalination plant.

*Why it matters:* Trump told reporters he would not resume major strikes after the midterms and mouthed "No" when asked again — while limited strikes continued without announcement.
