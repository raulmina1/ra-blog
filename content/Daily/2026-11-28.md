## ⚡ Brent posts its first weekly gain of November while WTI loses a fourth week in a row

Brent for January delivery fell \$1.13, or 1.2 %, to settle at \$95.44 on Friday but ended the week up 3.1 %, snapping three consecutive weekly losses; WTI for January lost 0.7 % to \$83.08 and 0.4 % on the week, a fourth straight weekly decline, leaving the Brent-WTI spread at \$12.36. The premium came from the Jazan refinery fire after Thursday's projectile — Aramco halted some operations — on top of Houthi claims on Ras Tanura and repeated tanker incidents, while Kpler puts Hormuz flows at 31 % of pre-war levels.

**Why it matters.** A rollover on Sunday keeps the geopolitical premium and leaves the structural surplus underneath it untouched, which is why PVM's \$88–97 range held all week. The quota fight is the more durable story: if the group cannot agree baselines, its pricing power weakens at exactly the moment two members are being courted out of it.

## In brief

### 🤖 AI & Models

**Both frontier labs now want a limit — and nobody has signed on**

On 22 November OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published pre-training progress data and proposed that all labs agree a common upper limit; on 25 November Dario Amodei argued on X that what must be restricted is AI autonomously building the next frontier model without human supervision.

*Why it matters:* Voluntary caps without an auditor are a procurement signal, not a rule: if labs slow pre-training, GPU rental and HBM lead times loosen first.

### ⚡ Energy & Markets

**The Bundestag passed the 2027 budget with €123 billion of new borrowing**

After months of coalition wrangling the Bundestag approved the 2027 federal budget on 27 November with core spending of €556 billion, about €31 billion more than this year, by 316 votes to 260 with two CDU deputies against and no abstentions.

*Why it matters:* A structurally higher deficit and a defence ramp-up at the same time as the ECB is about to hike is the combination that keeps Bund yields and the euro bid.

### 📊 Money & Policy

**The peso's official rate rose 1.08 % to 3,630.89 while business confidence fell again**

The COLCAP closed Friday up 1.51 % at 2,407.98, with Ecopetrol at 2,385 pesos (+1.06 %), ISA +2.79 % and Grupo Cibest +3.60 %, while Cementos Argos preferred shares hit a 52-week low.

### 🌍 Geopolitics & Conflict

**US forces disabled a 15th ship running the Iranian blockade**

US Central Command said a military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after multiple warnings, disabling its propulsion without injuring the crew; the ship had left Bandar Abbas.

*Why it matters:* Enforcement has become routine and undisputed, while the Iranian mine claims are unverified and repeated weekly.

**NRW funds a 10 MW electrolyser in Lichtenau with €11.2 million**

Westfalen Weser, the town of Lichtenau and its municipal utility began construction on the "Schlafender Riese" hydrogen project, funded with €11.2 million by North Rhine-Westphalia; NRW economics minister Mona Neubaur attended the symbolic groundbreaking.

*Why it matters:* This is the other half of the German ramp-up: small, regional, using existing pipes, and aimed at exactly the kind of industrial demand the core grid cannot reach economically.

<sub>stadt+werk · 20.11.2026 — [read the source](https://www.stadt-und-werk.de/k21-meldungen/projekt-schlafender-riese-gestartet/)</sub>

**The Albatros contract turns an auction win into €139.8 million of binding EU money**

Hy2gen Nordic signed the European Hydrogen Bank grant agreement for its Albatros project in Kassø, Denmark, on 16 November: up to €139.8 million, paid not as a lump sum but as a fixed premium of €0.97 per kilogram of certified renewable hydrogen over up to ten years.

*Why it matters:* The premium is the price signal that matters for German offtake: €0.97/kg is the cheapest binding support in the Danish window so far, against bids of 0.98 to 1.77 €/kg in the German-Danish AaaS round.
