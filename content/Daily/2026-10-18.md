## ⚡ Brent closes at \$109.86, its highest since 19 May — WTI up 10.4 % on the week as Hormuz flows fall to 68 % of baseline

Brent settled \$3.94, or 3.7 %, higher at \$109.86 on Friday — touching \$110 intraday for the first time since May — and WTI closed \$3.44, or 3.5 %, up at \$101.41; for the week Brent gained 4.9 % and WTI 10.4 %.

**Why it matters.** Crude is up more than 75 % this year and the buffer is gone: IEA puts observed inventories 509m barrels below pre-war levels with onshore stocks the lowest since at least 2016. That is the mechanism by which an oil shock becomes an inflation shock in Europe — the TTF move below is the same story in a different contract.

## In brief

### 🤖 AI & Models

**The AI build-out's price of money is now double digits: Anthropic offers Blackstone >10 % on the junior slice of a \$60bn chip package**

Anthropic is offering Blackstone a yield above 10 % to anchor the junior portion of the \$60bn debt package Broadcom is arranging to finance Google-designed TPUs for Anthropic, per the FT: Blackstone-managed funds would take \$9bn of an \$18bn junior tranche.

*Why it matters:* This is the IMF's circularity warning with a coupon attached: a company claiming positive free cash flow is paying distressed-adjacent yields to finance the chips it needs.

### ⚡ Energy & Markets

**TTF jumps 10.2 % in a week to €85.83/MWh as distribution demand rises 26 %, with German storage at 60.6 %**

The TTF front-month contract rose 10.2 % over the week to €85.83/MWh per a Standard Chartered note, with the front-month quoted at €87.54 on Friday evening and the THE month-ahead at €88.52.

*Why it matters:* The design flaw is now explicit in the government's own paperwork: the filling mandates made summer gas expensive, traders bought less.

### 📊 Money & Policy

**Wall Street's worst week since June: Nasdaq −4.5 %, chips down eight sessions in a row for the first time since 2008**

The Nasdaq fell 2.15 % to 26,140.03 on Friday and 4.5 % over the week — its worst week since June and the end of a four-week winning streak — while the S&P 500 lost 1.09 % to 7,659.44 (−1.95 % on the week.

*Why it matters:* The rotation is the signal: money left AI infrastructure and went into energy, utilities and defensives on the same day oil rose 3.7 %.

<sub>AP / WSJ / Yahoo Finance / TradingKey / Motley Fool · 16.10.2026 — [read the source](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-c27809783ae3230b44c26e5ee4bea056)</sub>

**The peso firms to COP 3,170.46 and De la Espriella calls the protests "98 % peaceful"**

The TRM for Friday 16 October was set at COP 3,170.46, down 27.19 pesos or 0.85 %, extending the strongest run of the year; La República's local benchmark print quoted Brent at \$105.77 and the policy rate stayed at 12.25 % after September's 25 bp hike.

*Why it matters:* A stronger peso against 6.3 % inflation and 12.25 % policy rate is a carry trade, not a recovery story — and it is happening while the IMF says the fiscal path is unsustainable.

<sub>La República / Superfinanciera / Noticias Caracol · 16.10.2026 — [read the source](https://www.larepublica.co/)</sub>

**The IMF says Colombia's public finances are "not sustainable" and opens the door to financing**

At the IMF's annual meetings in Bangkok, Western Hemisphere director Nigel Chalk said Colombia's primary deficit is "very large" at 3.5 % of GDP and public debt is around 60 % of GDP and still rising: "this situation is not sustainable or viable".

*Why it matters:* This is the first time the Fund has publicly attached the word "financing" to Colombia, which changes the price of every peso the state borrows and the politics of the 2027 budget.

<sub>El Espectador / Portafolio / Noticias Caracol / EFE · 16.10.2026 — [read the source](https://www.elespectador.com/economia/macroeconomia/fmi-advierte-que-las-finanzas-de-colombia-son-insostenibles-y-estudia-como-apoyarla/)</sub>

### 🌍 Geopolitics & Conflict

**Hormuz: the deadliest week of the war for shipping, and both sides now claim control of the strait**

BBC Verify, working from UKMTO incident reports, counted 12 vessels attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz between 5 and 11 October — the largest weekly number since the war began.

*Why it matters:* With reporting collapsing, the official count is now a floor, not a measure — which is why the physical evidence (Kpler flows at 68 % of baseline, tanker rates at records) matters more than either side's statements.
