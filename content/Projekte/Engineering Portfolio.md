## What I do today

I run **RA**, my own process-engineering and AI-automation consultancy based in Berlin (founded January 2026). The work runs on three fronts:

**Engineering as a service.** Process simulation (Aspen HYSYS, flash and PVT, mass and energy balances), preparation and review of P&amp;ID and PFD, mechanical and thermal calculations, and project technical documentation.

**Process automation with AI.** Python pipelines (Agents SDK, MCP servers) that connect plant data, spreadsheets and documentation under a **Human-in-the-Loop** principle: the agents run the routine, approval is requested on critical decisions.

**Data and knowledge.** Context services over knowledge graphs (GraphDB, SPARQL) and data pipelines for analysis and reporting (Power BI).

In development: **Process Design Workbench** — a single interface that combines numerical calculation (Polymath), process simulation (CAPE), P&amp;ID drafting and project scheduling (PDT).

## The workflow

Every project — whether it is a separator, an exchanger or a complete well-testing package — runs through the same five stages. On the left, the engineering path; on the right, the automation layer that feeds it:

<iframe src="/static/mental-maps/engineering-portfolio-workflow.html" style="width:100%;height:720px;border:1px solid #3a3a3a;border-radius:8px;background:#000" title="Engineering portfolio — project workflow"></iframe>

**1 · Process basis.** Scope, process data, constraints and applicable codes become a mass and energy balance: Aspen HYSYS simulations, PVT and flash behaviour, retention times and equipment duty.

**2 · Mechanical and thermal design.** Vessels and exchangers are sized and calculated — COMPRESS under ASME Section VIII Div. 1 &amp; 2, thermal design in Aspen EDR and cross-verified in HTRI. My own calculation sheets feed the first estimates and the final reconciliation.

**3 · Drawings and documentation.** The design becomes buildable: P&amp;ID and PFD, equipment and instrument datasheets, line lists, tie-ins, control philosophy.

**4 · Verification.** Flow assurance (hydrates, erosion, slugging), HAZOP, code check and client review. Findings go back to design — nothing is issued on trust.

**5 · Deliverable package.** Fabrication-ready drawings, signed calculation reports and datasheets — the documents a shop and a certifying authority can work from directly.

The automation layer runs alongside stages 1–3: Python and MCP agents collect plant data, fill sheets and assemble documentation, pausing for **human approval** wherever a decision carries technical or commercial weight.

> [!note] RA
> A design is finished when the shop can build it, the certifying authority can sign it, and the operator can run it — not when the drawing looks right.
